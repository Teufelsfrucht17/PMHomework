INTERPRETATION
A t-statistic is a coefficient divided by its standard error. The usual
two-sided 5% guide is |t| > 1.96. Borderline values are not decisive.
These are conventional OLS t-statistics; heteroskedasticity or serially
correlated errors can affect inference. Many simultaneous tests also invite
chance findings. A significant loading is association, not causation or skill.

Factors: MKT is the equity market return minus RF; SMB is small minus big
stocks; HML is high minus low book-to-market stocks (value minus growth);
MOM is past winners minus past losers. Positive loadings indicate exposure
in these directions; negative loadings suggest the reverse. These are
return associations, not direct measurements of portfolio holdings.

  SMB: +0.0621 (t=2.415), suggesting small-stock exposure;

In the scatter plots, dots nearer the 45-degree line indicate smaller
residuals. Stronger fits track that line more closely relative to each
index's own return variation; weak fits leave wide vertical scatter.
Panel ranges differ, so compare R2 as well as visual distances.
Ordinary R2 cannot decrease when predictors are added to nested OLS models
on identical data. Adjusted R2 penalizes extra predictors:
  adjusted R2 = 1 - (1 - R2) * (n - 1) / (n - k - 1),
where k is 1 for CAPM and 4 for the four-factor model (excluding intercept).
Its improvement supports better in-sample explanation after that penalty;
it does not demonstrate better out-of-sample prediction. Low R2 does not
prove skill or positive alpha: other exposures and strategy-specific risks
may remain. No time-varying-beta or autocorrelation models are estimated.
