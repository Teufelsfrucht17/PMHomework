INTERPRETATION
A t-statistic is an estimate divided by its standard error. It measures
how far the estimate is from zero in standard-error units. For a two-sided
5% test, |t| > 1.96 is an approximate guide, not a sharp proof of an effect.
Borderline values deserve caution. Conventional OLS inference assumes
constant error variance and uncorrelated errors; monthly fund returns may
violate these assumptions. Testing eight alphas also invites chance findings.

Aggregate Index: beta = 0.302 (t = 22.37). A 1 percentage-point increase in the
market excess return is associated with a 0.302 percentage-point increase in index excess return.
Monthly alpha = 0.151 percentage points (t = 2.48); it exceeds the approximate 5% threshold.
Alpha is the fitted excess return when market excess return is zero.
CAPM explains 66.3% of aggregate monthly excess-return
variation, leaving 33.7% unexplained by this factor.
