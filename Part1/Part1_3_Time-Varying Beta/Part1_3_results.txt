INTERPRETATION
Aggregate beta is 0.276 in up markets and 0.294 in down markets:
down-market exposure is higher by 0.019.
The interaction test gives t=0.439, p=0.6608; the difference is not statistically
distinguishable from zero at the conventional two-sided 5% level.
A 1 percentage-point fall in market excess return is associated with a
0.294 percentage-point fall in aggregate excess return
within down months, compared with 0.276 using the up-month slope.
This compares sensitivities, not average returns: each regime has its own intercept.
The point estimates suggest slightly less downside protection than the
up-market beta alone suggests; the economic size of this gap is small.
However, the sample does not provide clear statistical evidence of that asymmetry.

Other styles (difference = down beta minus up beta):
  Long/Short: 0.481 up, 0.511 down; difference +0.030, p=0.5895 (does not pass the 5% test).
  Market Neutral: 0.111 up, 0.077 down; difference -0.034, p=0.3394 (does not pass the 5% test).
  Growth: 0.510 up, 0.534 down; difference +0.024, p=0.7609 (does not pass the 5% test).
  Value: 0.562 up, 0.564 down; difference +0.002, p=0.9722 (does not pass the 5% test).

  FI Arbitrage: 0.133 up, 0.230 down; difference +0.097, p=0.0164 (passes the 5% test).
Largest numerical beta difference: Event-Driven; smallest: Macro.
Higher downside exposure with evidence at the 5% level: Event-Driven, FI Arbitrage.
This weakens their appeal as downside protection relative to their up-market exposure.
Macro's negative down-market beta suggests potential protection, but that
beta itself is not distinguishable from zero using the 1.96 guide.
The full-sample beta need not lie between the regime betas: its single
line also reflects differences in mean returns between the two regimes.
Economic size is the beta gap; statistical significance measures evidence
relative to estimation uncertainty. A small p-value need not imply a large gap.
A t-statistic is the estimate divided by its standard error. |t| > 1.96
is an approximate two-sided 5% guide; p(d) uses the finite-sample t distribution.
Two individually significant regime betas do NOT establish a significant
difference: the interaction coefficient d directly tests that difference.
Conventional OLS inference assumes constant error variance and uncorrelated
errors; violations can affect these tests. Eight tests also invite chance findings.
Generally, higher down-market beta means more market exposure when protection
is needed; lower down-market beta may offer more protection. Betas alone do
not measure total risk, tail losses, or overall investment attractiveness.
These are historical conditional associations, not causal effects or guarantees
of future downturn behavior. This is an up/down comparison, not a rolling beta.
