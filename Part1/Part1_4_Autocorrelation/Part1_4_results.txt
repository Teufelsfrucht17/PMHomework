QUESTION 4: INTERPRETATION
Aggregate Index: rho=0.1616, t=2.605, p=0.0097, 95% CI [0.0394, 0.2837].
A 1 percentage-point higher return last month is associated with a
+0.1616 percentage-point change in this month's fitted return.
Its AR(1) explains 2.6% of current monthly return variation.
Significant positive autocorrelation: Aggregate Index, Long/Short, Growth, Event-Driven, FI Arbitrage.
Significant negative autocorrelation: none.
No clear evidence of either at 5%: Market Neutral, Value, Macro.
The largest persistence estimate is FI Arbitrage: rho=0.429,
compared with 0.162 for the aggregate. Statistical significance
does not mean a strong fit: compare R-squared as well as the p-value.
The aggregate shows statistically detectable AR(1) dependence, but
the style-level results show whether that finding holds across strategies.
Long/Short is near the 5% threshold (p=0.0438); interpret cautiously.
Market Neutral is near the 5% threshold (p=0.0618); interpret cautiously.
Positive rho indicates persistence; negative rho indicates reversal. This
is own-return dependence, not a CAPM beta measuring market exposure.
A t-statistic is rho divided by its standard error. The p-value tests
H0: rho=0 against either sign; |t| > 1.96 is an approximate 5% guide.
These are unadjusted, individual tests; eight tests invite chance findings.
The positive findings are consistent with return smoothing, stale pricing
or exposure to less liquid assets, but AR(1) alone cannot establish a cause.
If economic gains/losses are spread across reported months, measured
monthly volatility can look lower and contemporaneous diversification
can look better than the underlying economic risk warrants. Annualizing
volatility by sqrt(12) can also understate risk when serial dependence persists.
These are index-level reported returns, not evidence about every individual
hedge fund. Aggregation, changing constituents and omitted exposures can matter.
Conventional OLS inference assumes constant error variance and uncorrelated
innovations; remaining dependence or heteroskedasticity can affect inference.
AR(1) tests only one linear lag: an insignificant result does not prove
independence. Historical persistence does not guarantee future behavior or
a profitable trading strategy, especially after costs and liquidity constraints.
