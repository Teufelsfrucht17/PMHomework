F01 - Four-factor interpretation overstates evidence about momentum profits
Priority: HIGH for the written conclusion
Type: CONFIRMED INTERPRETATION ISSUE; not a calculation error
Affected task: Part 2
Code: Part2/Part2_1_International Momentum.py, line 349
Saved report: Part2/Part2_1_results.txt, line 133
Numerical evidence:
  Incremental momentum alpha = +0.1269923 percentage points per month.
  Alpha t-statistic = 1.4340831; two-sided p-value = 0.1523240.
  MOM loading = +0.1132515; t = 5.7071267; p = 2.2298352e-08.
  R-squared of strategy-minus-equal-weight regression = 0.1004676 (10.05%).
  Unadjusted strategy-minus-equal-weight mean = +1.9249986 pp/year;
  mean-return t = 1.7803522, p = 0.0757642, so it is not significant at 5%.
Problem:
  The report says an insignificant residual alpha supports the conclusion that
  the model explains the profit in a 'statistical-alpha sense'. Failure to reject
  zero alpha does not establish zero alpha or prove complete factor explanation.
  The positive estimated momentum profit is also not significant at 5% under
  the conventional mean test. Distinguish that from the significant MOM loading.
Effect:
  A reader could infer a stronger answer to 'can the model explain the profit?'
  or 'are there momentum profits?' than these tests support.
Suggested replacement:
  'The strategy has a positive sample mean relative to equal weight, but that
  mean is not statistically significant at 5% under conventional OLS. MOM is
  significantly associated with the incremental return. After controlling for
  four factors, alpha is not distinguishable from zero (p=0.152), and the model
  explains about 10.05% of its monthly variation. This is compatible with a
  factor-based account of some returns, but does not prove zero residual alpha,
  complete explanation, or future momentum profitability.'
Required correction: revise this paragraph and regenerate Part2_1_results.txt.
No weights, returns, regressions or performance CSVs need correction.
