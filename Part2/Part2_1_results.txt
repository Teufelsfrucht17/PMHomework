DATA-DRIVEN CONCLUSION
Strategy: 12.24% annual arithmetic mean, 17.56% annual volatility,
Sharpe 0.557, maximum drawdown -60.57%. One unit grows to 37.19.
Equal weight: 10.31% annual arithmetic mean, 16.94% annual volatility,
Sharpe 0.464; final wealth 20.09.
Strategy minus benchmark annual arithmetic return: +1.92 percentage points.
CAGR is separately 11.22% for the strategy; it is a geometric
growth rate, not the annualized arithmetic average.
The momentum profit (strategy minus equal weight) averages 1.92 pp/year, with 6.30% annual volatility and a mean-return t-statistic of 1.78 (p=0.0758).
This benchmark-relative return isolates the effect of the momentum country weights.

For the complete funded strategy, four factors explain 59.3% of monthly excess-return variation
(adjusted R2=0.589).
  MKT (equity-market exposure): loading +0.919, t=22.76, p=2.48e-74: significant at 5%.
  SMB (small minus large stocks): loading +0.019, t=0.35, p=0.7231: not significant at 5%.
  HML (value minus growth stocks): loading +0.098, t=1.91, p=0.0574: not significant, borderline at 5%.
  MOM (past winners minus past losers): loading +0.064, t=1.73, p=0.0840: not significant at 5%.
Each loading is the associated percentage-point change in strategy excess
return for a one-percentage-point rise in that factor, holding others fixed.
Positive spread-factor loadings suggest the named exposure; negative loadings
suggest the opposite. Loading size and statistical significance are different.
Alpha is +0.0821 pp/month (t=0.50, p=0.6208).
Alpha is not statistically distinguishable from zero; that is not proof
that true alpha is exactly zero or that the model explains every return.
A positive raw return alone is not evidence of a distinct momentum profit.
The MOM loading of +0.064 measures momentum-factor association
after controlling for market, size and value; the strategy is long-only and
country based, while supplied Mom uses a different momentum construction.
Their returns therefore need not match.
